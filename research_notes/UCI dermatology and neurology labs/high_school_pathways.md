## 1. What UCI programs accept high school students for lab or summer research?

### Takeaway
UCI has no single "high school research" office. Access runs through about ten separate programs. For hands-on lab research in a biomedical field, the main routes are SCRIP (age 16+, the student must find their own UCI faculty sponsor first), the Cancer Research Institute's Youth Science Fellowship (rising seniors in Orange County; the 2027 cycle opened Nov 2, 2026), and the PERI Institute program. Brain Camp, Brain Explorer Academy, COSMOS, MedAcademy and the SOM Summer Online Research Program are paid, structured exposure programs rather than placements in a real lab. Most 2026 cycles are closed. Several 2027 cycles open between November 2026 and February 2027.

## 2. What are UCI's policies on minors in laboratories?

### Takeaway
UC system policy sets the minimums. Minors under 14 are barred from UC labs and shops except on approved, supervised tours. Older minors need a registered, approved activity, a sponsoring staff or faculty member, and a parent/guardian-signed registration form with a liability release. Minors may not handle controlled substances, radioactive materials or biohazards above BSL-2. UCI implements this through an EH&S "Visitors and Minors in Labs and Shops" program and a Minors Performing Research Registration Form. That document's full text could not be read in this session.

### Cited Findings
- UC policy "Minors in Laboratories and Shops": "Minors under the age of 14 are not permitted in University of California laboratories or shops, except when participating in an approved and supervised tour." Campuses "may develop more stringent policies and procedures." — [UC Policy, Minors in Laboratories and Shops (UCOP)](https://policy.ucop.edu/doc/3500602)
- Under the UC policy, minors aged 16 to 18 may not handle biohazardous materials requiring containment above BSL-2. Minors may not handle radioactive materials or radiation-producing machines; PIs may ask the campus EH&S Radiation Safety Officer for exceptions, which must be in writing. — [UC Policy (UCOP)](https://policy.ucop.edu/doc/3500602); summary in [UCLA EH&S, UC policies on lab safety](https://www.ehs.ucla.edu/news/uc-policies-on-lab-safety)
- "Minors are never permitted in any setting where research involving controlled substances is being performed," even if they are enrolled students. — [UC Policy (UCOP)](https://policy.ucop.edu/doc/3500602)
- A parent or guardian must sign the campus registration form, which includes a release of liability and hold-harmless agreement. The host department must keep the completed form for three years, or three years after the minor turns 18. — [UC Policy (UCOP)](https://policy.ucop.edu/doc/3500602)
- UCI EH&S program document: for ages 14 to 18, minors are allowed in lab settings only under specific conditions, such as being enrolled in a listed course or part of an approved program. A staff employee sponsoring a non-student minor "must certify adherence to this policy and local implementing procedures on the Minors Performing Research Registration Form." — [UCI EH&S, Visitors and Minors in Labs and Shops Program (PDF)](https://www.ehs.uci.edu/programs/_pdf/lab-res/visitors-minors-in-labs-and-shops.pdf)
- School of Medicine SCRIP layer: high school interns must be over 16, need a UCI Parental Authorization for Minors Form, and under-18s may not work with highly hazardous materials, controlled substances or certain carcinogens. — [UCI SOM SCRIP Compliance SOP (PDF)](https://medschool.uci.edu/sites/default/files/2025-11/research-compliance-sop-scrip_aug-2025.pdf)
- Related UCI pages: Risk Services "Working with Minors" (campus youth-protection requirements) and the Office of Research page on volunteers in animal labs. — [UCI Risk Services, Working with Minors](https://risk.uci.edu/minors/index.php); [UCI Office of Research, Volunteers in Animal Labs](https://research.uci.edu/animal-care-and-use/policies-and-guidance/volunteers-in-animal-labs/)
- Human-subjects research involving minors as subjects falls under separate IRB rules. That is distinct from minors acting as researchers. — [UCI Office of Research, Vulnerable Populations](https://research.uci.edu/human-research-protections/subject-enrollment/vulnerable-populations/)

### Inferences
- In practice, a 14–15-year-old is very unlikely to get a UCI wet-lab placement outside a structured program. SCRIP, the main SOM route, starts at 16.
- Because a PI must sponsor, certify and file paperwork for any minor, a cold email offering dry-lab work (data analysis, literature review, image annotation, coding) removes most of the lab-safety burden. Dry-lab work does not involve hazardous materials in a lab space.

### Gaps
- The UCI EH&S PDF could not be opened, so UCI's exact rules for ages 14–15 versus 16–17, the required EH&S safety training modules (e.g., UC Learning Center lab safety courses), and the current form names and links are unverified. Search metadata suggested the EH&S document and UC policy are several years old (UCOP policy roughly 2013; EH&S PDF about 4 years). They may still be current but should be checked.
- Whether UCI requires a Live Scan background check, TB test or immunizations for high school SCRIP interns was not found.

## 3. What do faculty and credible sources recommend for high school students cold-emailing professors?

### Takeaway
University guidance is mostly written for undergraduates but applies directly. Research the lab first. Write a short, specific email in your own voice that references the professor's actual work. Attach a resume. Ask for a conversation rather than a job. Send one polite follow-up after 1–2 weeks, then move on. Expect a low response rate. For minors at UCI specifically, the email should show you know the sponsor and age rules (for example, "I'm 16 and understand SCRIP requires a faculty sponsor").

### Cited Findings
- Read the professor's lab page and recent papers before writing. Mentors respond to students who are curious and enthusiastic about their specific work. — [Duke Undergraduate Research, Getting Started](https://undergraduateresearch.duke.edu/getting-started-research)
- Be selective: detailed, specific emails to three professors beat identical emails to ten. — [NYU Psychology, Undergraduate Research FAQs](https://as.nyu.edu/departments/psychology/undergraduate/research-opportunities/undergraduate-research-faqs.html)
- Keep it short. The cold email "is not a place to narrate your life story." — [Penn Career Services blog](https://careerservices.upenn.edu/blog/2023/11/16/to-reach-out-or-not-to-reach-out/)
- Use a subject line that states your interest, a one-line introduction, and a request to meet. — [Illinois Physics, Getting Undergraduate Research](https://publish.illinois.edu/phystudentadvisoryboard/physics-graduate-school/getting-undergraduate-research/)
- Engage the faculty member with questions about their research rather than bluntly asking to work in their lab. — [Princeton PCUR, Advice on Reaching Out to Faculty](https://pcur.princeton.edu/2017/11/24/advice-on-reaching-out-to-faculty/)
- A first contact rarely produces a position, so ask about opportunities or for a conversation. If there's still no reply after a follow-up, move on. — [Duke Undergraduate Research](https://undergraduateresearch.duke.edu/getting-started-research)
- Attach a resume or CV rather than listing everything in the body. Frame limited experience as eagerness to learn. Mention relevant training (e.g., human-subjects/CITI) if the lab does human-subjects research. — [Columbia TC IRB blog, How to Find a Lab and Cold Emailing Tips (2025)](https://www.tc.columbia.edu/institutional-review-board/irb-blog/2025/how-to-find-a-lab-and-cold-emailing-tips-an-irb-perspective/); [Michigan Tech, Mentor Email Guidelines (PDF)](https://www.mtu.edu/biological/research/undergraduate/pdfs/mentor-email-guidelines.pdf)
- A University of Toronto professor warns that overly polished, generic text (including LLM-generated text) reads as spam. Write in your own voice. — [Michael Guerzhoy (U of T), Cold Emails](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~guerzhoy/advice/coldemails.html)
- More step-by-step templates: [Cornell HECEC, Step-by-Step Guide on Cold Emailing Professors](https://hecec.human.cornell.edu/2022/12/05/step-by-step-guide-on-cold-emailing-professors/); [MSU Denver, Tips for Emailing Professors](https://www.msudenver.edu/undergraduate-research-creative-scholarship-program/for-students/research-opportunities/mentored-research/tips-for-emailing-professors/)
- Response rates (anecdotal, non-university sources): one high school student reported about 200 emails and 2 positive responses. Advice sites estimate roughly 1–2 positive replies per 10 tailored emails. Many professors don't reply because they lack time to mentor an intern. — [Polygence blog](https://polygence.org/blog/how-to-cold-email-professors); [Empowerly](https://empowerly.com/blog/how-to-email-professors-for-research-opportunities); [MedSchoolCoach](https://www.medschoolcoach.com/planning-ahead-high-school/); [CollegeVine Q&A](https://www.collegevine.com/questions/27846/emailed-a-professor-and-i-have-not-gotten-back-their-reply). These are commercial or anecdotal sources, so treat them as low-reliability.
- UCI's own UROP FAQ says students "do not sign up for research through UROP; research is a relationship between you and your faculty mentor." This confirms that direct faculty outreach is how UCI expects placements to start. (UROP itself serves UCI undergraduates.) — [UCI UROP FAQ](https://urop.uci.edu/frequently-asked-questions/)
- UCI SCRIP suggests finding a sponsor through school counselors, science teachers, and outreach events where faculty are present. Warm introductions are an officially suggested alternative to cold email. — [UCI SOM, SCRIP](https://medschool.uci.edu/research/office-research/policies-and-guidelines/student-research-internship-program)

### Inferences
- Tailoring for UCI: say your age and grade up front. Name the program route (SCRIP if 16+, or "YSFP applicant") so the PI knows the paperwork path exists. Offer dry-lab or computational tasks (literature review, data cleaning, image analysis in dermatology or neuroimaging, coding) as a low-burden starting point. Ask for a short call or meeting.
- Labs whose "Join Us" pages say they don't take high schoolers outside structured programs (Burke, Nicholas) should not be cold-emailed for ad-hoc positions. Check each lab's page first.
- Dermatology at UCI is a clinical department in the SOM, so a dermatology PI would sponsor through SCRIP. The Ganesan lab is one documented example of a dermatology lab that has hosted high schoolers.
- I found no faculty-written guide aimed specifically at high school students. The cited guidance is undergraduate-oriented, adapted here by inference.

### Gaps
- No rigorous data on cold-email response rates for high school students was found. Only anecdotes exist.
- No UCI faculty-authored guidance on emailing professors as a minor was found.
- Most guides don't say explicitly whether to attach a resume or include it as a link. The cited guides favor attaching one.
